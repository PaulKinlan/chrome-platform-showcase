v160 · webrtc · practical: phone menu keypad

An IVR keypad on sender.dtmf

The product job DTMF exists for: pressing keys during a call to drive a phone menu, enter a conference PIN or dial an extension. This keypad is wired to a real negotiated loopback call. Every key calls insertDTMF() on the standard RTCDTMFSender, appending to whatever is still queued, and every tonechange event is reported as it happens.

Dial string and timing

Allowed: 0–9, A–D (a–d are normalised to upper case), #, * and comma (a 2-second pause). Anything else makes insertDTMF() throw InvalidCharacterError — try it, the real call runs either way.

why the keypad appends

insertDTMF() replaces the tone buffer. The spec says that to add tones while others are still queued, call it with the remaining toneBuffer plus the new tones — which is exactly what each key does here (dtmf.insertDTMF(dtmf.toneBuffer + key, …)). Hammer a few keys quickly and watch the buffer grow, then drain one tone per tonechange. “Cancel queued tones” calls insertDTMF(""): the tone already playing finishes, the rest are dropped.
