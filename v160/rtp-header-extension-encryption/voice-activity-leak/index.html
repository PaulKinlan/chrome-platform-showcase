Voice activity leak

Every outgoing audio packet in a WebRTC call carries the ssrc-audio-level header extension (RFC 6464): how loud you are, per packet. SFUs use it to pick the active speaker. Without cryptex it sits in the RTP header in plaintext, so anyone on the network path can chart when you talk, even though the audio itself is encrypted. This page runs a real call, "talks" with a tone, and shows the per-packet level the receiver reads. Then it compares three protections: cryptex, nothing, and dropping the extension with setHeaderExtensionsToNegotiate().

Flag. Cryptex needs the RtcRtpHeaderEncryptionPolicy feature: chrome://flags/#enable-experimental-web-platform-features, or launch with --enable-blink-features=RtcRtpHeaderEncryptionPolicy, --enable-features=RtcRtpHeaderEncryptionPolicy or --enable-experimental-web-platform-features. The meter shows what the receiving app decodes after decryption; whether an on-path observer could read the same numbers comes from the negotiated SDP, which the wire-status line reports.

the trade-off

Dropping the extension hides the level from observers but also from your SFU and your own speaker UI (the meter goes blank). Cryptex keeps the feature and hides it: the SFU, which terminates SRTP, still decrypts it, but a passive observer of the UDP flow does not. That is the design goal of RFC 9335 and why the explainer offers it by default. Combining both is the belt-and-braces path for apps that do not need audio levels at all.
