reading the map

  • Index n means “the gap before character n”; index = length is the gap after the last character.
  • \Z on "end\n\n" only matches at 4 and 5: it lets one terminator sit between it and the end, never two.
  • On "a\r\nb\r\n", \Z matches at 4, 5 and 6: before the final \r\n pair, between \r and \n (the lone-\n alternative is followed by the end), and at the end itself. A global replace with \Z can therefore split a CRLF pair — worth knowing before using it in replaceAll.
  • The desugared forms use RegExp modifiers (?-m:…) (Chrome 125+). They work even where the new escapes do not, which is what makes a transpiling fallback possible.
