Light dismiss improvements for popovers and dialogs

"Light dismiss" — click outside a popover (or a <dialog closedby=any>) and it closes — used to trigger on the pointerdown+pointerup pair. That let right clicks and completed drag gestures dismiss, and on touch screens could kill a popover the user was only trying to scroll past. The improvement moves the trigger to the click event: gestures that never complete a click — scrolls, drags, secondary buttons — no longer count. These demos measure which model your browser runs from real gestures, and bust a myth along the way: even the legacy pair model waits for release, not press.

concepts

  1. Dismissal phase probe

    Open a popover (and a closedby=any dialog), then press outside — the page timestamps every pointerdown/pointerup/click against the toggle/close moment and renders the dismissal timeline. Measured myth-bust: even the legacy pair model waits for your release; the models' real difference is gestures that produce no click, which this instrument flags as the legacy signature when they dismiss.

  2. Scroll-gesture survivor

    The motivating bug, measured rather than asserted: a popover open over a scrollable feed. Drag the feed with touch — the instrument classifies each gesture (tap / drag / cancelled-into-scroll) and reports whether the popover survived on your engine, distinguishing genuine click-model behavior from pair-model builds that merely escape via pointercancel.

  3. Right-click & drag lab

    The other two fixed triggers: a right click outside (secondary button never produces a click, so the new model ignores it) and a press-drag-release text selection (a drag past the click threshold produces no click either). Per-gesture verdicts from real events.

why it shipped

Users kept losing popovers they were trying to keep: touching a page to scroll it counts as pointerdown, so any touch scroll instantly closed open popovers; right-clicking to open a context menu did the same. Bug reports converged on the same root cause — light dismiss listened to the raw pointer pair instead of the higher-level click, which browsers already synthesise only for completed primary-button activations. Switching the underlying trigger to click fixes scroll gestures, drags, and secondary buttons in one move, and aligns popover and closedby=any dialog behavior.

enabling it

In developer trial as of Chrome 145 behind LightDismissFromClick (experimental in runtime_enabled_features.json5; shipping is tracked for Chrome 154): launch with --enable-blink-features=LightDismissFromClick or enable chrome://flags/#enable-experimental-web-platform-features. No API surface changes — the probes here detect the model purely behaviorally, which is also why they work on every engine.
