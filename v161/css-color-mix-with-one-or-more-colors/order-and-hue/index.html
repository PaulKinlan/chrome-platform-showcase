Argument order and hue

With two colours, swapping the arguments and the percentages gives the same result. With three or more in a polar space it does not. This page renders all six orderings of three colours and counts how many different colours come out.

Ships in Chrome 161. Before that, enable chrome://flags/#enable-experimental-web-platform-features (there is no dedicated flags entry), or launch Chrome/Canary with --enable-blink-features=CSSColorMixVariadic or --enable-experimental-web-platform-features. How it works

The question: does reordering the arguments change the colour? The spec says it does in polar spaces. Chrome mixes the first two colours, then mixes that result with the third. In oklch, lch, hsl and hwb the hue is interpolated along the shorter (or chosen) arc, and which arc is shorter depends on the hue of the intermediate result. In rectangular spaces such as oklab, lab and srgb the result is a plain weighted average, so order cannot matter.

The page renders the six permutations of A, B, C, computes each one and counts the distinct results. A hue method keyword (longer hue, increasing hue and so on) applies to every pairwise step.

Practical rule: when a design token mixes three or more brand colours in a polar space, its argument order is part of the design. If you need order-independent mixing, use oklab.

Findings

Tested 2026-11-26 on Chrome for Testing 160.0.8184.0 with --enable-experimental-web-platform-features, using red, lime and blue. In oklch the six orderings give two different hues: red, lime, blue computes to oklch(0.648794 0.288568 145.258) (green) and blue, red, lime to oklch(0.648794 0.288568 25.2582) (orange-red). Lightness and chroma are identical; only the hue moves, by 120°. longer hue flips red, lime, blue to hue 25.26. In oklab all six orderings agree to the sixth decimal (one ordering differs in the last digit from floating-point rounding). Mixing blue with white and grey in oklch kept blue’s hue (264.05), because white and grey have a powerless hue that takes the other colour’s value. Chrome 158: rejected; Chrome 161: not verified.
