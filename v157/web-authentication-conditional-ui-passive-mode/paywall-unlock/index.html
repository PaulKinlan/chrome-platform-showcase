Paywall unlock

The explainer's first example, built for real: a reader lands on a paywalled article from a social link, with an account but no active session. The page fires a passive request on load — no sign-in form anywhere — and the browser quietly offers the reader's passkey. Taking it unlocks the article in place; ignoring it costs nothing. Readers without a passkey see no prompt at all and keep the normal "Sign in" path.

Behind a flag — no shipping milestone yet. Enable chrome://flags/#web-authentication-ambient-signin and relaunch, or launch with --enable-features=WebAuthenticationAmbientSignin, then create a passkey (button below or on the passive request page) and reload. Demo honesty: there is no publisher server here — the returned assertion is decoded in the page and its challenge is checked, but its signature is not verified. A real site posts cred.toJSON() to its server first.

The sign-in nobody had to look for

Most readers who could sign in never do. They are mid-scroll, arrived from a link, and the "Sign in" button lives in a header they never look at. Every extra step between them and their account is a reason to bounce.

Subscribers keep reading. If you have an account, your browser may already be offering it — no form needed.

Continued for subscribers

Passive mode moves the moment of sign-in from "go find the button" to "the browser noticed you have an account here". The suggestion is non-modal and only appears when a credential exists, so readers without one never see an empty prompt — and the site never learns whether they have an account until they choose to use it.

async function armPassive() {
  const caps = await PublicKeyCredential.getClientCapabilities();
  if (!caps.conditionalPassiveGet) return;   // keep the normal header link only
  passive = new AbortController();
  const cred = await navigator.credentials.get({
    mediation: 'conditional', uiMode: 'passive',
    publicKey: await fetchOptions(), signal: passive.signal,
  });
  await verifyOnServer(cred); unlockArticle();
