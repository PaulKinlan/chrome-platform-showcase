Anchor playground

^ and $ change meaning when you add the m flag: they start matching at every line. \A, \z and \Z never do — they always mean the start and end of the whole input. Edit the text, flip the m flag, and watch all three anchorings run side by side on the same input.

what to notice

  • With m on, ^foo$ finds foo on line 1 and line 3. \Afoo\z finds neither unless foo is the entire input.
  • \Afoo\Z also accepts "foo\n" and "foo\r\n" — exactly one trailing line terminator — but not two.
  • Turn m off and ^…$ behaves like \A…\z: buffer boundaries make that explicit and independent of the flag, so you can mix line anchors and input anchors in one regex.
