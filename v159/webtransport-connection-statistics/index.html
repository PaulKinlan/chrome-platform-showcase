WebTransport connection statistics

WebTransport.getStats() answers the questions a transport layer usually keeps to itself: round-trip time (minRtt, smoothedRtt, rttVariation), an estimated sending rate when one is available, and the datagram loss ledger — expired outgoing, lost outgoing, dropped incoming. The stats stay readable even after the connection closes. Chromium does not currently expose connection byte or packet totals.

concepts

  1. getStats probe — the exact surface: is getStats on WebTransport.prototype, and what does a real call return on a session that never connects? Promise, shape, and rejection behaviour, verbatim.
  2. Live stats dashboard — point it at a real WebTransport server (a public echo server by default), connect, send datagram traffic, and poll getStats() into a live RTT / send-rate / loss readout.
  3. Stats after close — the spec's parting gift: statistics remain accessible after the connection closes. Close (or fail) a session, then keep calling getStats() and watch what still answers.

why it shipped

Without transport stats, apps rating their own connection quality had to guess from application-level timings. getStats() (from the WebTransport spec's stats section) exposes what QUIC already measures — RTT and loss — so adaptive-bitrate media, multiplayer games, and telemetry can react to the real network. The W3C shape is a promise-returning method resolving a WebTransportConnectionStats dictionary; Chromium ships the RTT trio, a nullable estimatedSendRate, and datagrams counters first.
