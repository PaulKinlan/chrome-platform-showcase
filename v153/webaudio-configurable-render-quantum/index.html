WebAudio: Configurable render quantum

Web Audio has processed sound in fixed 128-frame blocks since day one. The new renderSizeHint context option changes that: pass an integer for a specific quantum, "hardware" to let the UA match the device's optimal buffer, or "default"/omit for the classic 128. The resulting size is readable as context.renderQuantumSize and inside every AudioWorkletProcessor.

concepts

  1. Hint & probe

    The exact surface — and its classic detection trap: constructing with {renderSizeHint: 256} "succeeds" even on engines that ignore unknown dictionary members, so the honest probe is 'renderQuantumSize' in BaseAudioContext.prototype plus reading the value back. Both run live, with the trap demonstrated for real.

  2. Worklet quantum meter

    Ground truth from inside the engine: an AudioWorkletProcessor measures the actual frames per process() call in an OfflineAudioContext constructed with your chosen hint, and posts the measurement back. 128 on today's engines; your hint (rounded per spec) on Chrome 153.

  3. Latency budget planner

    Why you'd change it: block size ⇄ callback rate ⇄ latency. Pick a quantum and sample rate to see the real block duration and callbacks-per-second, next to your device's actual AudioContext.baseLatency and sampleRate read live.

why it shipped

128 frames (2.9ms at 48kHz) is a compromise: pro-audio apps want smaller blocks for lower latency, while battery-sensitive playback wants bigger blocks and fewer wakeups — and hardware often runs at 96, 192, or 240-frame buffers that force the UA to re-buffer around Web Audio's 128. Letting the app hint the render size (and letting "hardware" defer to the device) removes that mismatch. The worklet contract keeps everything honest: process() block length and renderQuantumSize always tell you what the engine actually chose.
