Underrun Stress Lab

Why would you ever pick a 1024-frame quantum? Throughput. This lab plays a quiet 110 Hz tone from an AudioWorkletProcessor while you crank a synthetic per-sample DSP load and optionally jam the main thread. Live meters track whether the render thread keeps real time: the render deficit (wall-clock elapsed minus audio rendered) stays near zero while the graph keeps up, and grows — audibly stalling — when process() is too slow. Run the same load at 128 and at a large quantum and compare the recorded runs.

Measurement honesty: an AudioWorkletGlobalScope has no performance.now() — only Date.now(), with millisecond resolution. And audio is pulled in bursts: the output device requests a hardware buffer's worth of quanta back-to-back, then waits — so gaps between individual process() calls are bursty on every device and are not glitches. That is why this lab measures the render deficit instead: wall-clock elapsed minus audio time rendered. Keeping up ⇒ deficit bounded near one hardware buffer; too slow ⇒ deficit grows and playback audibly stalls. A "stall episode" is a deficit crossing 50 ms (hysteresis at 25 ms) — an honest underrun proxy, not the OS driver's XRun counter. Note what it shows: main-thread jank should not grow the deficit (the render thread is independent), while heavy in-worklet DSP load eventually does — and the load threshold where stalling starts is higher with a larger quantum, because per-callback overhead is amortised.
