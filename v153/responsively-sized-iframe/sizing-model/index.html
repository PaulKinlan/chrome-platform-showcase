Sizing model: values, writing modes, constraints

frame-sizing accepts five keywords: auto | content-width | content-height | content-block-size | content-inline-size. Each non-auto keyword takes one dimension from the child's natural size (here an opted-in child measuring exactly 480×360) while the other dimension resolves normally — and the logical keywords follow the embedding element's writing mode, not the child's. The intrinsic size then feeds standard replaced-element sizing, so max-width, max-height and aspect-ratio still apply on top. Drive all of it live below.

what this probes

The five keywords are the complete grammar of the property definition (initial auto, discrete animation, applies to replaced elements). The readout reports getComputedStyle(iframe).getPropertyValue("frame-sizing") — an empty string means the browser did not parse the property at all, which is reported as unsupported rather than glossed over. Every “reading” is derived from measured geometry; nothing is asserted that was not observed.
