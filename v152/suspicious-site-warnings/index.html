Suspicious site warnings

Chrome 152 adds a second tier of Safe Browsing warning for users of Enhanced protection: sites with signals of potential maliciousness get a bypassable pop-up warning the user must click through — in addition to the red interstitial that continues to guard confirmed malicious sites. This is browser UI, not a web API: pages get no signal that a warning was shown. These demos are honest about that — they explain, recreate with clear labels, and give site owners the real tools.

concepts

  1. Warning tiers explorer

    Pick a protection level (Enhanced / Standard / No protection) and a site-reputation scenario (clean / suspicious signals / confirmed malicious) — the explorer shows which warning the user would meet, as a clearly-labelled recreation, and why the new bypassable tier only exists under Enhanced protection.

  2. Site-owner toolkit

    What a page can do: nothing at runtime (there's deliberately no web-exposed signal — a live probe on the page proves the absence), but plenty before that. Type your domain to generate real Google Safe Browsing Transparency-Report and Search-Console links, and work an interactive checklist of the signals that get sites flagged.

why it shipped

The red interstitial requires confirmed badness — by the time a site is on that list, victims have often already visited. Enhanced-protection users share more real-time data with Safe Browsing precisely to get earlier, probabilistic protection; the suspicious-site pop-up is that promise made visible: a softer, bypassable friction for "signals say be careful" that doesn't need to wait for confirmation, while the interstitial keeps its meaning. There is no API surface: exposing "a warning was shown" to the page would leak the user's protection level and Safe Browsing's verdicts to the very sites being warned about.

seeing it

Requires Chrome 152+ signed into chrome://settings/security → Enhanced protection, then visiting a site Safe Browsing considers suspicious — which is not something a demo can (or should) arrange. The explorer concept recreates both warning tiers with explicit "recreation" labels; nothing here simulates a verdict as if it were real.
