ReadableStream + Opus encoder pipeline

The explainer floats a future startRenderingStream(). You can build it today: wrap an indefinite-length context in a ReadableStream whose pull() calls startRendering() and whose cancel() calls close(), then pipe the chunks into a WebCodecs AudioEncoder. The question: does the stream's backpressure really stop the audio graph from rendering ahead of a slow consumer, and does cancelling the stream really stop the context?

How it works

  1. Source. new ReadableStream({ pull, cancel }, new CountQueuingStrategy({ highWaterMark })). The stream calls pull() only while its queue holds fewer than highWaterMark chunks, and each pull() renders one 3,840-frame chunk (80 ms at 48 kHz, 30 render quanta, so Chrome does not round it).
  2. Sink. A WritableStream wraps each AudioBuffer as f32-planar AudioData and hands it to an AudioEncoder configured for Opus. Encoded packets are counted, not kept.
  3. Stop. When the target length is reached, or you press cancel, the page aborts the pipe. The abort cancels the readable side, which runs cancel(), which awaits ctx.close(). Any render still in flight rejects with AbortError.

"Max rendered ahead" is rendered chunks minus chunks the sink has finished with, measured each time a chunk is rendered. If backpressure works it never exceeds highWaterMark (or 1 when highWaterMark is 0, because a waiting reader still asks for one chunk), however slow the consumer is.

Findings

Chrome for Testing 158.0.8127.36, headless, no flags, 2026-11-27. Backpressure held exactly: with a 20 ms consumer, 5 s of audio (63 chunks) never rendered more than 1 chunk ahead at highWaterMark 1, and never more than 16 ahead at highWaterMark 16 (78 rendered, 63 consumed when the target was reached; the 15 surplus chunks were discarded by the cancel). highWaterMark 0 behaved like 1, because the pipe's waiting read still triggers one pull(). Cancelling, by reaching the target or by the button, left the context "closed", and a render still pending at close() rejected with AbortError ("Audio context is going away"). Opus output was 11.9 to 12.4 KB per audio second at the 96 kbit/s target. With no consumer delay the graph rendered 2 s of stereo audio in about 90 ms, roughly 22 times faster than real time.
