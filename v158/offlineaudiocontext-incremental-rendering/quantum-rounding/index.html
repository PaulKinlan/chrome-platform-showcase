Quantum rounding and seam test

This page asks two falsifiable questions on the build you are running: does any chunk come back larger than chunkSize, and when you glue the chunks back together, is the result sample-for-sample identical to a one-shot render?

How it works

The audio graph only ever advances in whole render quanta (128 frames in Chrome; the spec's renderSizeHint for choosing another size is not exposed in Chrome 158). A chunk boundary that falls inside a quantum can either be honoured by slicing the quantum, or rounded to the end of it. Experiment 1 shows which one this build does. The only chunk that is not a whole number of quanta is the last one of a finite context, which is cut at length.

If you need a hard memory ceiling, pass a multiple of 128. If you need exact chunk lengths (say, 20 ms Opus frames at 48 kHz, 960 frames), request a multiple of 128 and re-slice the output yourself.

Findings

Chrome for Testing 158.0.8127.36, no flags, 2026-11-27, at 8,000 and 48,000 Hz:

  • startRendering(0) throws NotSupportedError: "The requested chunk size cannot be zero."
  • Any other chunkSize is rounded up to a multiple of 128: 1 and 127 return 128, 129 returns 256, 300 returns 384, 1000 returns 1024. The sample rate does not change this. This is larger than the explainer's "maximum number of samples given by chunkSize". Recorded on the friction ledger; no Chromium issue found for it yet.
  • A chunkSize at or above the remaining frames returns the remainder (4,000) and closes the context.
  • The chunked render matched the one-shot render exactly: with chunkSize 300 on 4,000 frames, chunks of 384 × 10 + 160, maximum absolute difference 0.
